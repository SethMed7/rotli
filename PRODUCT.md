# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

macOS desktop (Tauri, with a browser twin for safe development and regression testing)

## Users

Rotli is for people who want to capture, connect, and revisit knowledge without
handing ownership of their files to a service. The primary user works quickly
from a Mac and expects the interface to disappear around writing; a secondary
workflow lets that same person use local agents and explicit connected tools
without weakening the file or privacy boundary.

## Product Purpose

Rotli is a calm, local-first workspace over one user-owned vault. It makes
Markdown notes and conventional editable work surfaces immediately useful,
keeps capture friction low, and lets people choose whether the on-device
Librarian adds organization metadata that improves retrieval and chat context.
Rotli's layer never turns projections, AI state, or application metadata into a
second source of truth.

## Positioning

Rotli is the durable workspace where user-owned files remain the product, not
an export from it.

## Brand Personality

Calm, warm, and durable. Rotli is direct without feeling clinical, friendly
without becoming playful chrome, and confident enough to stay out of the way.

## Anti-references

Rotli must not resemble a cloud dashboard, a glowing AI control room, a card
catalog, or a decorative whiteboard wrapped around static previews. Avoid
attention-seeking motion, glass and shadow effects, raw provider vocabulary,
and novel controls where a familiar desktop affordance communicates better.

## Design Principles

- Keep the user's content visually and architecturally primary.
- Make capture immediate and advanced capability progressively discoverable.
- Preserve one understandable source of truth across every view and tool.
- Earn trust through explicit state, reversible actions, and honest boundaries.
- Prefer familiar keyboard-safe desktop behavior over ornamental novelty.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Every workflow must remain keyboard reachable, expose visible focus, preserve
readability and semantic state in all four environments, support reduced
motion, avoid color-only meaning, and remain usable in narrow desktop windows
and with increased operating-system text settings.
